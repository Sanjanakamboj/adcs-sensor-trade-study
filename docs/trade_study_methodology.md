# Milestone 3 Trade-Study Methodology

This document describes the resource model, cost/complexity scoring,
normalization, weighted trade matrix, weight-sensitivity/robustness study,
break-even analysis, hard requirements, and recommendation logic used by
`src/adcs_sensor_trade/resource_model.py`, `cost_complexity_model.py`,
`trade_metrics.py`, `normalization.py`, `trade_matrix.py`, and
`requirements.py`, and executed end-to-end by
`scripts/run_trade_study.py`.

**Read this before treating any Milestone 3 number as more than
illustrative.** Milestone 3 does NOT select a vendor product, does NOT
fabricate commercial pricing, does NOT implement a gyro/EKF/closed-loop
attitude estimator, and does NOT claim flight-qualification for anything.
It builds a transparent, reproducible WEIGHTED TRADE MATRIX on top of the
accepted Milestone 1 (instantaneous geometry/information) and Milestone 2
(operational availability/cadence) results, plus illustrative resource and
cost/complexity assumptions.

All numbers quoted in this document were regenerated by
`scripts/run_trade_study.py` and cross-checked against
`results/m3_report.txt`, `results/m3_raw_metrics.csv`,
`results/m3_weighted_scores.csv`, and `results/m3_monte_carlo_summary.csv`.

## 1. Resource assumptions (`resource_model.py`)

Every resource number is tagged with a `ValueSource`:
`m1_m2_modeled` (computed from the accepted M1/M2 modules),
`illustrative_assumption` (a representative engineering value, not a
vendor spec), or `derived` (arithmetic combination of the above).

Individual component baselines (all `illustrative_assumption`):

| Component | Units | Mass [kg] | Avg power [W] | Peak power [W] | Volume proxy [cm^3] | Interface burden [1-5] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Star tracker | 1 | 0.45 | 1.5 | 2.5 | 250 | 4 |
| Sun sensor (per head) | 2 | 0.05 | 0.2 | 0.3 | 20 | 2 |
| Magnetometer | 1 | 0.15 | 0.3 | 0.4 | 40 | 2 |

Rationale:

- **Star tracker (0.45 kg, 1.5 W avg, 2.5 W peak):** representative order
  of magnitude for a small/mid-class star tracker in the "tens of
  arcsec" precision class implied by the Milestone 1 sensor model (30
  arcsec 1-sigma), including its optical head, baffle, and dedicated
  star-identification processing electronics. NOT tied to any specific
  vendor product.
- **Sun sensor (0.05 kg, 0.2 W avg, 0.3 W peak, x2 units):** a digital
  coarse/fine Sun sensor of the class implied by the Milestone 1 model
  (0.5 deg 1-sigma, 60 deg FOV half-angle) is a small, simple, low-power
  commodity part. Two units are baselined for broader angular coverage
  (a single 120 deg-FOV head cannot see the full sky), consistent with
  common smallsat practice, though this trade study's M1/M2 geometry and
  availability analysis (which drives the accuracy/availability/cadence
  metrics) still uses the single-head FOV model from Milestone 1/2 for
  consistency with the accepted results - the SECOND unit here only adds
  resource burden, it is not separately modeled for coverage in the
  availability timeline (a documented conservative simplification: the
  resource cost of broader coverage is counted, but no additional
  availability credit is claimed for it).
- **Magnetometer (0.15 kg, 0.3 W avg, 0.4 W peak):** representative
  fluxgate-class magnetometer mass/power, consistent with the Milestone 1
  model's illustrative field magnitude and noise level.
- **Interface burden (1-5 ordinal):** star tracker = 4 (precision
  boresight alignment, baffle keep-out geometry, thermal stability);
  Sun sensor = 2 and magnetometer = 2 (simple analog/digital interfaces,
  straightforward mounting, with the magnetometer's boom/magnetic-
  cleanliness accommodation adding a small amount of burden).

**Architecture totals are DERIVED, never hardcoded.** The Sun+mag
architecture's mass/power/volume/unit-count totals are the literal sum of
the Sun-sensor (x2) and magnetometer (x1) component profiles above:

| Architecture | Mass [kg] | Avg power [W] | Peak power [W] | Units | Interface burden |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Star tracker | 0.450 | 1.500 | 2.500 | 1 | 4.0 |
| Sun + magnetometer (derived) | 0.250 | 0.700 | 1.000 | 3 | 2.0 |

(0.250 kg = 2 x 0.05 kg + 1 x 0.15 kg; 0.700 W = 2 x 0.2 W + 1 x 0.3 W;
1.000 W peak = 2 x 0.3 W + 1 x 0.4 W - see
`resource_model.sunmag_architecture_totals()`.)

In this illustrative model, Sun+mag has a real mass and average-power
advantage over the star tracker (about 0.20 kg and 0.80 W lower) - this is
reported and used honestly in the trade matrix, not hidden or inflated.

## 2. Cost/complexity scoring rationale (`cost_complexity_model.py`)

NO dollar figures appear anywhere. All scores are normalized ordinals in
{1, 2, 3, 4, 5} (1 = favorable/low burden, 5 = unfavorable/high burden).
"Cost" (`procurement_cost`) is kept distinguishable from "complexity" (the
mean of the other five ordinals).

| Sub-score | Star tracker | Sun+mag | Rationale (see full text in `cost_complexity_model.py` docstrings) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Procurement cost | 4 | 1 | Precision optics/detector/star-ID processing chain is comparatively expensive and longer-lead-time vs. commodity Sun sensors/magnetometers. |
| Integration complexity | 4 | 2 | Boresight alignment, baffle keep-out geometry, and thermal stability requirements vs. simple panel/boom mounting. |
| Calibration burden | 3 | 2 | Precision optical calibration mostly done at the vendor factory (moderate burden on the integrating team) vs. a small number of well-understood bias/scale-factor/boresight-offset calibration parameters. |
| Software complexity | 2 | 3 | Turnkey vendor star-ID algorithm + simple quaternion interface vs. needing an explicit two-vector attitude-determination algorithm (TRIAD/QUEST) plus rank/conditioning-aware logic. |
| Operational constraints | 4 | 4 | Sun/Earth/Moon keep-out geometry constrains attitude planning for the star tracker; eclipse + Sun-sensor FOV geometry constrains WHEN Sun+mag can obtain a full-attitude update - comparable operational planning burden from different root causes. |
| Fault-management burden | 3 | 2 | Commonly single-string on smallsats, needing a defined fallback mode, vs. three simple largely-independent units that degrade gracefully to a rank-2/coarse mode. |

Derived sub-scores:

- `cost_score` = `procurement_cost`: star tracker = **4.0**, Sun+mag = **1.0**.
- `complexity_score` = mean of the other five: star tracker =
  (4+3+2+4+3)/5 = **3.2**, Sun+mag = (2+2+3+4+2)/5 = **2.6**.

These reflect standard smallsat ADCS engineering judgment (a star tracker
being a comparatively expensive, integration-intensive, single-string
component vs. a commodity, low-cost, gracefully-degrading Sun+mag suite)
and were fixed BEFORE running the weighted trade matrix - they were not
adjusted afterward to manufacture a particular winner.

## 3. Metric extraction (`trade_metrics.py`)

`trade_metrics.py` is the single source of truth: every M1/M2-derived
number in the trade matrix is computed by calling
`availability.run_availability_timeline`,
`availability.summarize_boolean_series`,
`availability.compute_full_attitude_update_cadence`, and
`availability.star_tracker_usable_updates` against
`trade_metrics.baseline_availability_config()` - which is exactly
`OrbitAvailabilityConfig(dt_s=1.0, n_orbits=1.0)`, the SAME baseline
configuration used by `scripts/verify_availability.py`. Nothing is
hand-typed from the M1/M2 reports; `tests/test_trade_metrics.py` contains
tight-tolerance regression tests confirming the extracted values match the
accepted headline numbers.

Extracted raw metrics (from `results/m3_raw_metrics.csv`):

| Condition number cond(J) | 1.0 (isotropic, by construction) | 1.903 (median, while full-rank & available) |
| Full-attitude observable | True | True (when available) |

The Sun+mag accuracy/conditioning numbers (0.6729 deg / 1.903) are the

range (median cond 1.903, P95 2.045, max 2.047).

## 4. Normalization (`normalization.py`)

Every metric has a documented `MetricSpec`: direction
(higher-is-better / lower-is-better), method, and clip bounds. Both
methods used here apply the same linear-with-clip transform; what differs
is what the clip bounds MEAN:

| Category | Direction | Method | Clip bounds | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | lower better | absolute requirement min-max | [0, 2.0] deg | 0 deg is the physical ideal; 2.0 deg is an illustrative "still usable but poor" coarse 3-axis attitude-knowledge ceiling, chosen independently of either architecture's actual value. |
| Availability | higher better | absolute requirement min-max | [0, 1] fraction | Physical bound on a fraction of orbit - no judgment call needed. |
| Cadence | higher better | absolute requirement min-max | [0, 2.0] Hz | Illustrative ceiling for a determination-only full-attitude update cadence for this mission class. |
| Mass | lower better | absolute requirement min-max | [0, 3.0] kg | Illustrative ADCS sensor-suite mass ceiling for a representative ~50-150 kg smallsat bus. |
| Power | lower better | absolute requirement min-max | [0, 5.0] W | Illustrative ADCS sensor-suite average-power ceiling for the same bus. |
| Cost | lower better | fixed ordinal map | [1, 5] | 1 and 5 are the scale's own fixed, universally-anchored endpoints, not derived from comparing the two architectures. |
| Complexity | lower better | fixed ordinal map | [1, 5] | Same fixed-anchor rationale as cost. |

**We deliberately avoid a naive relative "winner gets 1.0, loser gets
0.0" min-max** anywhere in the production trade-matrix code path, because
with only two architectures being compared, that method would silently
launder ANY difference (however small) into a maximal utility gap - see
`normalization.normalize_relative_minmax_for_reference`, kept only as a
documented counter-example exercised by a dedicated test
(`test_naive_relative_minmax_produces_the_winner_take_all_artifact`), and
never called by `trade_matrix.py`.

Resulting normalized utilities at baseline (from
`results/m3_normalized_utilities.csv`):

| Architecture | Accuracy | Availability | Cadence | Mass | Power | Cost | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Star tracker | 0.9958 | 0.8730 | 0.8731 | 0.8500 | 0.7000 | 0.2500 | 0.4500 |
| Sun+mag | 0.6635 | 0.2318 | 0.5794 | 0.9167 | 0.8600 | 1.0000 | 0.6000 |

## 6. Hard requirements (`requirements.py`)

A SEPARATE set of representative, illustrative pass/fail requirements,
evaluated independently of the weighted score. Resource ceilings are
deliberately OMITTED as hard requirements - there is no defensible
mission-independent mass/power cutoff the way there is for e.g. a minimum
usable-availability fraction; resource pressure is already captured (as a
preference) in the weighted trade matrix.

| Requirement | Threshold | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Worst-axis attitude-knowledge error | <= 2.0 deg | A basic 3-axis-stabilized smallsat needs at least coarse (better than a couple of degrees) 3-axis attitude knowledge to be a physically meaningful ADCS solution. |
| Full-attitude availability fraction | >= 30% of orbit | Representative requirement that an absolute update be obtainable often enough to periodically bound/reconverge an attitude filter that otherwise propagates on gyro data alone between absolute updates. |
| Longest continuous outage | <= 600 s (10 min) | Representative bound on how long an attitude filter may propagate on gyro data alone before accumulated drift becomes a concern for a modest-precision gyro. |
| Full-attitude update cadence | >= 1.0 Hz | Representative minimum cadence for absolute updates to usefully bound estimator drift, independent of the separate closed-loop control-bandwidth question (explicitly deferred, see `docs/availability_methodology.md`). |

These thresholds were chosen for physical plausibility BEFORE evaluating
which architecture would pass or fail them. Result (from
`results/m3_report.txt`): the **star tracker passes all 4**; **Sun+mag
fails 2 of 4** (availability and longest-outage duration) - reported
honestly, not adjusted to avoid a failure.

## 7. Weighted trade matrix result (baseline)

| Architecture | Total weighted score |
|---|---|
| Star tracker | 0.7636 |
| Sun + magnetometer | 0.6635 |

**Winner (baseline): star tracker**, margin +0.1001. The largest single
contributor to the margin is the **availability** category (+0.1282 of
the +0.1001 total, i.e. availability alone would be a larger star-tracker
lead than the net margin - other categories partially offset it, notably
mass/power/cost which favor Sun+mag).

## 8. Decision robustness (part F)

**5 named scenarios** (from `results/m3_weighted_scores.csv`):

| Scenario | Star tracker | Sun+mag | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.7636 | 0.6635 | Star tracker |
| accuracy_priority | 0.8564 | 0.6490 | Star tracker |
| resource_constrained | 0.7730 | 0.7773 | **Sun+mag (flips)** |
| availability_priority | 0.7872 | 0.5610 | Star tracker |
| cost_complexity_priority | 0.5730 | 0.7462 | **Sun+mag (flips)** |

**2 of the 4 non-baseline scenarios flip the recommendation.**

**Monte Carlo weight-space sampling**: 20,000 weight vectors drawn
uniformly over the 7-category simplex via `rng.dirichlet(alpha=ones(7))`
with `numpy.random.default_rng(seed=42)` (fully reproducible - re-running
`scripts/run_trade_study.py` reproduces bit-identical fractions). Score
difference is defined consistently as (star tracker score) - (Sun+mag
score).

- Fraction favoring star tracker: **56.29%**
- Fraction favoring Sun+mag: **43.71%**
- Fraction tied: 0.00%
- Score-difference distribution: mean +0.0179, median +0.0225,
  std 0.1479, range [-0.6526, +0.5591].

A 56/44 split (and 2 of 4 named scenarios already flipping) is reported
here as **FRAGILE / MISSION-PRIORITY-DEPENDENT**, not robust. (This
document's own working definition, used consistently in the script and
report: a >=90% split in either direction is called "robust"; anything
less is called "mission-priority-dependent".)

## 9. Break-even analysis (part G)

All break-even points are found by numeric bisection
(`scipy.optimize.brentq`) on the baseline-weighted score margin, holding
all other metrics/weights at baseline:

| Break-even variable | Current value | Break-even value | Meaningful? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sun+mag availability fraction | 23.18% | **73.21%** | Yes - would require roughly tripling Sun+mag's modeled availability. |
| Star-tracker mass | 0.450 kg | **2.451 kg** | Yes - star tracker would need to be >5x heavier than modeled here. |
| Star-tracker average power | 1.500 W | **4.835 W** | Yes - star tracker would need to draw >3x its modeled power. |
| Availability-category weight (others scaled proportionally) | 0.20 | **0.0521** | Yes - dropping availability's weight below ~5.2% of the total flips the decision toward Sun+mag. |
| Sun+mag worst-axis-error improvement (holding all else fixed) | 0.6729 deg | **no crossing found** | **No** - even an idealized near-zero-error Sun+mag does not, by itself, flip the baseline decision. Reported honestly as a non-meaningful lever, not forced. |

## 10. Recommendation logic (part I)

The final recommendation (generated verbatim by
`scripts/run_trade_study.py`, reproduced in the README) explicitly and
separately states: (1) hard-requirement compliance per architecture,
(2) the baseline weighted score result, (3) the sensitivity/robustness of
that result (5-scenario + Monte Carlo), and (4) qualitative risks not
captured numerically - most importantly that BOTH architectures need an
(unmodeled) gyro to propagate attitude between absolute updates, and
Sun+mag's much longer/more-frequent outages place materially more burden
on that unmodeled gyro's drift performance than the star tracker's short,
rare exclusion outages. Because 2 of 4 alternative priority scenarios
already flip the winner and the Monte Carlo split is only 56/44, the
recommendation is explicitly stated as CONDITIONAL, not absolute.

## 11. Limitations

- All Milestone 1/2 modeling limitations still apply unchanged (see
  `docs/geometry_and_information.md` and
  `docs/availability_methodology.md`): isotropic star-tracker noise
  model, constant-magnitude tilted-dipole field DIRECTION (not a full
  IGRF model), idealized nadir-pointing attitude profile, cylindrical
  eclipse shadow, no J2/drag, no gyro-propagated estimator.
- **No gyro/EKF/closed-loop simulation** is implemented anywhere in this
  project. The gyro-propagation dependency is discussed only as a
  system-level qualitative risk (see Q11 in `results/m3_report.txt`).
- Resource (mass/power/volume) and cost/complexity numbers are
  ILLUSTRATIVE engineering assumptions for a representative small/mid
  smallsat, not vendor datasheet values, procurement quotes, or
  flight-heritage claims.
- The normalization clip bounds (2.0 deg accuracy ceiling, 2.0 Hz cadence
  ceiling, 3.0 kg mass ceiling, 5.0 W power ceiling) are themselves
  illustrative engineering judgment calls, documented above and in
  `normalization.py`; a different, still-defensible choice of bounds
  could shift (though, given the size of the accuracy/availability/
  resource differences observed here, likely not qualitatively reverse)
  the baseline numeric margin.
- The weighted trade matrix answers "which architecture better serves a
  STATED set of mission priorities", not "which architecture is
  universally superior" - the whole point of Sections 8-9 above is that
  the answer is priority-dependent.
- No vendor/product selection, no fabricated commercial pricing, and no
  flight-qualification claim is made anywhere in this project.
