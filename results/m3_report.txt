7. ANSWERS TO MILESTONE 3 QUESTIONS
==============================================================================

  Q1. Which architecture passes the representative hard requirements?
      Star tracker: PASSES ALL requirement(s).
      Sun+mag:      FAILS at least one requirement(s) (min_full_attitude_availability_fraction, max_longest_outage_s).

  Q2. Which has the higher baseline weighted score?
      Star tracker (0.7636 vs. 0.6635).

  Q3. What drives the score difference?
      The 'availability' category contributes the largest share of the star_tracker-minus-sun_plus_mag margin (+0.1282 of +0.1001 total).

  Q4. Illustrative resource-model mass/power for each architecture?
      Star tracker: 0.450 kg, 1.500 W avg (2.500 W peak).
      Sun+mag:      0.250 kg, 0.700 W avg (1.000 W peak) - DERIVED by summing 2 Sun-sensor + 1 magnetometer component(s).

  Q5. Does the star tracker's M1 accuracy advantage still matter after availability/resources are folded in?
      Its accuracy-category weighted contribution (0.1992) is real but is NOT the largest driver of its lead in the baseline weighting - 'availability' contributes more to the margin. The star tracker's baseline win is driven primarily by its operational-availability/continuity advantage (and to a lesser extent cadence), not its raw instantaneous-accuracy advantage alone.

  Q6. Does Sun+mag's resource advantage matter relative to its availability limitation?
      Sun+mag DOES have a real illustrative resource advantage (lower mass and average power) in this model, but its mass+power weighted-contribution advantage (+0.0340) is smaller in magnitude than its availability-category disadvantage (-0.1282) at baseline weights - the resource advantage does not offset the availability gap here.

  Q7. How often does the recommendation change across the 5 named weighting scenarios?
      2 of 4 non-baseline scenarios flip the recommendation relative to the baseline winner (Star tracker) (resource_constrained and cost_complexity_priority favor Sun+mag; accuracy_priority and availability_priority favor the star tracker, matching baseline).

  Q8. Monte Carlo weight-space sampling: what fraction favors each architecture?
      Star tracker: 56.29%   Sun+mag: 43.71%  (n=20000, seed=42).

  Q9. What parameter/weight changes flip the recommendation?
      Sun+mag availability rising to ~73.2% (from 23.2%); star-tracker mass rising above ~2.45 kg (from 0.45 kg); star-tracker average power rising above ~4.84 W (from 1.50 W); or the availability-category weight dropping below ~5.2% (from the baseline 20%) while other weights scale up proportionally. Improving Sun+mag accuracy ALONE does not flip it (see part 6).

  Q10. Is the recommendation robust or mission-priority-dependent?
      FRAGILE / MISSION-PRIORITY-DEPENDENT: the Monte Carlo split is 56.3/43.7, and 2 of 4 named alternative-priority scenarios already flip the winner - this is reported honestly as a MISSION-PRIORITY-DEPENDENT result, not a universal one.

  Q11. Qualitative risks not captured numerically:
      - Gyro-propagation dependency: BOTH architectures rely on a gyro (not modeled
        here) to propagate attitude between absolute updates; Sun+mag's much longer
        and more frequent outages place a materially heavier burden on that
        (unmodeled) gyro's drift performance than the star tracker's short, rare
        outages - a system-level consideration this score does not capture.
      - All M1/M2 sensor/orbit/environment models are illustrative simplifications
        (isotropic star-tracker noise, constant-magnitude tilted-dipole field
        direction, idealized nadir-pointing profile, cylindrical eclipse shadow),
        not flight-fidelity or vendor-specific models.
      - No vendor/product selection or qualification claim is made anywhere.
      - No closed-loop control-bandwidth analysis: 'cadence' here means usable
        ABSOLUTE full-attitude update cadence, not a control-loop rate requirement.
      - The resource and cost/complexity numbers are illustrative engineering
        assumptions, not procurement quotes or datasheet values.
******************************************************************************
8. FINAL RECOMMENDATION
******************************************************************************
  Hard requirements : Star tracker passes all 4 representative hard requirements; Sun+mag fails 2 of 4 (availability and longest-outage duration).
  Weighted score    : At baseline mission-priority weights, Star tracker scores higher (0.764 vs. 0.664).
  Robustness        : 2 of 4 alternative named priority scenarios flip the decision, and the Monte Carlo weight-space split is 56.3% star tracker / 43.7% Sun+mag - this result is MISSION-PRIORITY-DEPENDENT, not overwhelming.
  Qualitative risk  : both architectures need an (unmodeled) gyro for propagation between updates; Sun+mag's far larger/longer outages place materially more burden on that propagation than the star tracker's short, rare exclusion outages.

  RECOMMENDATION: For a representative smallsat with BASELINE (balanced) mission priorities, select the STAR TRACKER as the primary 3-axis attitude-determination sensor - it passes every representative hard requirement, has the higher baseline weighted trade score, and its advantage is driven primarily by operational availability/continuity rather than raw instantaneous accuracy alone. CONDITIONAL CAVEAT: this recommendation FLIPS to the Sun sensor + magnetometer suite for a mission whose priorities are genuinely mass/power-constrained or cost/complexity-constrained (see the resource_constrained and cost_complexity_priority scenarios above), and the overall Monte Carlo robustness (56/44 split) means this is a mission-priority-dependent recommendation, not an absolute one.
