6. ANSWERS TO MILESTONE 2 QUESTIONS
==============================================================================
  Q1. Fraction of orbit each architecture can do a full attitude observation:
      Star tracker = 87.30%   Sun+mag = 23.18%
      (Sun sensor ALONE = 23.18% of orbit geometrically visible, but a
       single Sun-sensor vector is only rank-2 - it never gives full attitude
       by itself; magnetometer is modeled as always geometrically 'available'
       but likewise only rank-2 alone.)

  Q2. Longest-outage root cause:
      Star tracker: sun_exclusion (369.0 s)
      Sun sensor:   eclipse (3939.0 s)
      Sun+mag:      eclipse (3939.0 s)

  Q3. Fraction of Sun+mag 'geometrically usable' time that is poorly conditioned:
      0.00% of the full-rank-usable time (cond(J) > 10.0).

  Q4. Effective Sun+mag full-attitude update cadence:
      6723.0 updates/orbit (1.1589 Hz effective),
      vs. star tracker direct 10130.0 updates/orbit (1.7462 Hz).

  Q5. Star-tracker availability sensitivity to exclusion angle:
      monotonically non-increasing, from 100.0% at 5 deg down to 50.0% at 90 deg.

  Q6. Sun+mag availability sensitivity to eclipse fraction (beta) and FOV:
      Widening FOV from 15 to 179 deg raises Sun+mag availability from 0.0% to 64.2%.
      Raising beta from 0 to 70 deg drops eclipse fraction from 36.7% to 0.0%, and (in this nadir-pointing/velocity-
      boresight geometry) Sun+mag availability moves from 23.3% to 0.0% (FOV-geometry coupling can
      dominate the eclipse effect at high beta - see docs/availability_methodology.md).

  Q7. Does the magnetometer's higher rate alone materially improve full-attitude
      update availability? NO - confirmed above: usable updates/orbit stayed at
      6723.0 across magnetometer rates 5-50 Hz. This is BY
      CONSTRUCTION: a magnetometer measurement alone is rank-2 (geometry.py), so no
      amount of extra magnetometer samples recovers the missing 3rd degree of
      freedom - only a second, non-collinear, AVAILABLE vector (the Sun sensor,
      whose own eclipse/FOV-limited availability is the true bottleneck) can.

  Q8. Stronger operational availability/continuity (availability + quality ONLY,
      not mass/power/cost): STAR TRACKER - 87.30% available with 369 s longest outage,
      vs. Sun+mag at 23.18% available with 3939 s longest outage. The star
      tracker's ONLY outage driver in this model is Sun-exclusion geometry; Sun+mag
      additionally loses availability to eclipse and Sun-sensor FOV geometry.

  Q9. What limitations prevent M2 alone from making the final sensor-suite
      recommendation?
      - No mass, power, or cost modeling (a star tracker is typically heavier,
        more power-hungry, and more expensive than a Sun sensor + magnetometer).
      - No full gyro-propagated estimator (MEKF/EKF) - outages here are treated as
        instantaneous measurement gaps, not propagated through a real attitude
        filter with process noise and gyro drift between updates.
      - No closed-loop ADCS/control-bandwidth analysis of what update cadence is
        actually SUFFICIENT for a given mission's pointing requirements.
      - Illustrative orbit/attitude/eclipse/field models (circular orbit, fixed
        nadir-pointing profile, cylindrical shadow, tilted-dipole field direction),
        not high-fidelity ephemeris or a full IGRF geomagnetic field model.
      - No vendor/product-specific hardware qualification claims.
